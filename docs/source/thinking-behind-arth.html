The thinking behind Arth

A thumbprint proves who signed. Arth checks that the borrower understood.
Artificial intelligence is the tool. Real intelligence is the brain.

The problem

A thumbprint proves who signed a loan. It does not prove the borrower understood it. The RBI's Key Facts Statement circular (RBI/2024-25/18, 15 April 2024) asks lenders to give the KFS in a language the borrower understands, explain it, and take an acknowledgement that the borrower understood. For someone who cannot read, that acknowledgement can still be a mark on a page they cannot read. Arth (अर्थ, from the Sanskrit artha: meaning, and wealth) is built for that gap. It checks, out loud, whether the borrower understood what is on the paper, before they sign.

The idea

1

Everything is said out loud

Every screen speaks its own instruction in the borrower's language, with replay and slow buttons. Nobody has to read or type.

2

Teach-back, not tick-box

The borrower explains the loan in their own words. Open questions come in random order.

3

One engine, three doors

In person with the agent, online with the lender's KFS PDF or QR, and a call check on a synthetic sales call.

4

A shared receipt

A PDF of what was checked, with fingerprints of the paper and every clip. Not a signature, not a loan approval.

Which models

What we researched

The rule behind the gap

The RBI Key Facts Statement circular (RBI/2024-25/18, 15 Apr 2024). It decided which terms we check: loan amount, money in hand, instalment and how often, processing fee, tenure and late fee.

The Gnani APIs, read and tested

Prisma takes at most 30 seconds per clip and reads the format from the file name, so the browser encodes its own 16 kHz WAV. Prisma v3.0 returns numbers as digits. Only one request runs at a time, so every call shares one queue with retry. Timbre needs numbers written as words.

A real Hindi and English test

A spoken sample in the Gnani playground showed that the Hindi "nahi" comes after the field name: आपको 30,000 का loan मिलेगा। EMI 2666 और processing fee नहीं लगेगी। That exact output became a unit-test fixture.

The other entries

We looked at what others in the challenge built, to be sure we were solving a different problem: understanding, not explaining or scam-spotting.

The law on voice

Voice is not a legal e-signature in India; recordings can be evidence, and hashes help. So the wording is always "proof of understanding, alongside the signature".

Who we designed for

Someone who may not read the paper at all, helped by an agent, a family member or an SHG didi. That is where the zero-reading design and the helper mode come from.

Consent, privacy and security, built in

✓
Consent comes first.

In agent mode the agent records a spoken consent line before anything about the loan.

✓
Recording is always visible.

Recording starts only on a tap, with a red pill and timer on screen. Nothing records in the background.

✓
Every clip is labelled.

Agent, borrower or helper, so it is clear who said what.

✓
The borrower stays in control.

Any answer can be said again. There is no typing anywhere.

✓
One button wipes it.

"सब मिटाएँ" clears the whole session. No login, no database: nothing is kept.

✓
The key never leaves the server.

The Gnani key lives only in server settings, never in the browser or in Git. Audio and document text are never logged.

✓
Hardened against misuse.

Rate limits and size caps on both voice routes, which accept only the models, voices and languages the app uses. Security headers on every page.

✓
Not a signature.

Voice is proof of understanding, alongside the signature.

Foul play we planned for

From the agent

Verbal mis-selling ("no fee"), coaching the answers, "the app says it's fine". Answered by the field-by-field agent check, random teach-back questions and no verdict words.

From either side

"Nobody explained", or a disputed recording. Answered by speaker labels, the agent's consent clip, and SHA-256 fingerprints of the paper and every clip on one receipt shared with both sides.

Out of scope, said openly

An agent and a borrower working together to fool the check. And Arth does not prove whose voice it heard.

The thinking behind it

Why it is built this way

Voice-first, zero reading

The borrower may not read. Every screen speaks, buttons are large, and status is colour plus icon plus sound: ✅ chime, ❌ buzz, ❔ grey.

Teach-back, not a quiz

Explaining in their own words shows more than tapping "yes". Open questions come in random order, including "what happens if you miss an instalment?"

No LLM decides anything

Money terms cannot be guessed. Fixed rules with 553 unit tests make every comparison traceable to evidence.

Unknown stays unknown

A missing value is "not mentioned", never zero. A different unit or frequency (weekly vs monthly) is "couldn't check", never a conflict.

Money as pictures

Amounts are shown as notes and coins with digits (₹1,500 = 3 × ₹500), and spoken as words.

Careful words

Matched, different, not mentioned or couldn't check, plus the question to ask. Never "safe", "approved", "verified" or "fraud".

What we chose not to do

No AI judgeNo OCRNo voice biometricsNo voice cloning No face recognitionNo APR unless validatedNo real dataNo legal-signature claim

The thinking behind it

From plan to product: what changed, and why

LoanLens → Arthname

The plan was called LoanLens. The product became Arth (अर्थ): one word that means both "meaning" and "wealth", and is understood across many Indian languages (अर्थ · অর্থ · અર્થ).

Camera OCR droppedpaper input

Reading a photo needs a third-party AI model. Arth reads text-based KFS PDFs and a QR code with plain code instead; "lenders print one QR on the KFS".

Teach-back made exactthe gate

Checked point by point: money in hand, instalment and how often, fee, and what a missed instalment costs. A wrong number re-explains only that point; three tries on one point stops the flow.

More than Hindilanguages

Hindi and English are stable, Marathi is under native review, and seven more Gnani languages are listed as Beta but cannot be selected until a native speaker checks the wording.

Instant, still realspeed

Fixed prompts were generated once with real Timbre calls and stored, so screens speak at once. Anything else is spoken live; all listening is live Prisma.

Share to Arthphone

Installed on an Android phone, Arth appears in the share sheet, so a KFS PDF received on WhatsApp opens straight in the app.

Security passbefore judging

Key and Git history audited (no leaks), voice routes locked to the app's own settings, test pages hidden, security headers added, dependencies patched.

Honest limits

A prototype
Accuracy has not been measured on real borrowers, so no accuracy figure is claimed. Hindi is the most tested flow. Arth does not prove whose voice it heard, and it checks only the listed terms. All data is synthetic, and the call check runs on a synthetic call only.
Built with Gnani Prisma v3.0 (listens) and Gnani Timbre v2.5 (speaks). Plain rules decide.
